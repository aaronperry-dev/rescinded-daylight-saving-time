How this is made & sources

Scenarios. Current law applies today’s DST rules. Rescinded keeps every zone on standard time all year (Arizona, which never observes DST, is unchanged). Permanent DST keeps clocks at today’s summer time all year.

Sunrise & sunset. For every place and every day of the pipeline computes sun times (NOAA approximation, about a minute accurate, standard 90.833° zenith), adds the scenario’s UTC offset for that day, and maps the latest or earliest clock time that place ever sees. Bands are 30 minutes wide; lines are isochrones labelled in local clock time (no AM/PM; see the legend). Lines jump at time-zone borders. Under current law the latest sunrise often falls on the morning of the March clock change rather than in January, so rescinding DST changes it by only minutes and permanent DST moves it by less than a full hour in places such as Los Angeles or Denver. The earliest sunset is identical under current law and rescinded DST.

Data. Zone boundaries: Timezone Boundary Builder release (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). DST rules: IANA tz database via Python zoneinfo. State outlines: Natural Earth. Map: Leaflet; tiles © OpenStreetMap contributors © CARTO / Esri.
