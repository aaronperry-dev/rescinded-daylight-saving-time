How this is made & sources

Scenarios. Current law applies today’s DST rules. Rescinded keeps every zone on standard time all year (Arizona, which never observes DST, is unchanged). Permanent DST keeps clocks at today’s summer time all year.

Sunrise & sunset. For every place and every day of the pipeline computes sun times (NOAA approximation, about a minute accurate, standard 90.833° zenith), adds the scenario’s UTC offset for that day, and maps the latest or earliest clock time that place ever sees. Bands are 30 minutes wide; dashed lines are isochrones labelled in local clock time (no AM/PM; see the legend). Lines jump at time-zone borders. Under current law the latest sunrise often falls on the morning of the March clock change rather than in January, so rescinding DST changes it by only minutes and permanent DST moves it by less than a full hour in places such as Los Angeles or Denver. The earliest sunset is identical under current law and rescinded DST.

Basemap. Rendered by the pipeline, with no tile server and no API key: land is coloured by elevation in feet above sea level and the ocean and lakes by depth in feet, from NOAA’s ETOPO global relief model (public domain), with a hillshade. Coastlines and lakes: Natural Earth (public domain). Boundaries: heaviest line is an international border, the thin black line a state or province, and the thick white line with a dark edge a time-zone border.

Data. Zone boundaries: Timezone Boundary Builder release (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). DST rules: IANA tz database via Python zoneinfo. Sunrise and sunset colours: ColorBrewer YlOrRd and RdPu schemes, © Cynthia A. Brewer, Pennsylvania State University. Map: Leaflet. The optional OpenStreetMap and Esri basemaps load from their own servers.
